\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Independent context encoding.}
Fusion-in-Decoder~\citep{izacard2021fid} encodes retrieved passages
independently in the encoder and fuses them in the decoder cross-attention.
Parallel Context Windows~\citep{ratner2023pcw} realizes an analogous idea in
a decoder-only model without training, by partitioning positions into
independent windows and merging them at generation time. Contextual Position
Encoding for Extrapolation (CEPE)~\citep{yen2024cepe} adds a trained adapter
for parallel context encoding at long context lengths. Our work is the
decoder-only characterization of the block-diagonal mask family under
train-free inference on the single-hop parity axis; direct comparison
against PCW is out of scope for this preprint
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Fixed-pattern sparse attention.}
Sparse Transformers~\citep{child2019sparse},
Longformer~\citep{beltagy2020longformer}, and
BigBird~\citep{zaheer2020bigbird} sparsify attention using patterns fixed by
position: local windows, strided offsets, random blocks, and global tokens.
These patterns are agnostic to the document boundaries in a RAG prompt: a
Longformer window straddles two retrieved documents just as readily as it
sits inside one. DDI sparsifies along the boundary the retriever already knows
about, which can enable cache reuse under the position-layout assumptions
studied here.

\paragraph{Attention sinks.}
StreamingLLM~\citep{xiao2024streamingllm} shows that transformer language
models allocate substantial attention mass to the first few tokens of a
sequence, and that these ``sinks'' are load-bearing. A block-diagonal mask
gives each document its own first tokens: $N$ sinks compete for the role
that a single sink held under full causal attention. This is a diagnostic
lens for interpreting the parity gap of \texttt{ddi\_strict}, in which
only a single bridge row carries cross-document information.

\paragraph{Practical caching alternatives.}
PagedAttention~\citep{kwon2023pagedattention} reduces the memory cost of KV
caches under existing (dense) attention and enables sharing of exact-match
prefixes. It is the practical alternative to DDI for the caching claim
(Section~\ref{sec:results-caching}); we compare rather than compete.
DDI's addressable case is broader --- any repeated document at any position
--- but requires a different attention pattern, so the two are complementary
rather than substitutes.

\paragraph{Controllable-$N$ evaluation.}
The Lost-in-the-Middle protocol~\citep{liu2023lostmiddle} manipulates the
position of a gold document within a variable-length distractor set, and
shows that models degrade non-monotonically with position. We adopt the same
design as our controllable-$N$ probe, since it isolates the dimension
(number and position of documents) that DDI most directly changes.

\paragraph{Differentiators.}
Relative to the above, DDI is (i) a decoder-only train-free
characterization at 7--8B parameters on retrieval QA; (ii) a
measurement of the parity margin between \texttt{full}, \texttt{ddi\_qf},
and \texttt{ddi\_strict} rather than a binary work/does-not-work test of
a single independent-context design; and (iii) an explicit report of the
gap between analytical and realized prefill speedup, which prior work
does not quantify.

\paragraph{RoPE position schemes.}
Rotary Position Embeddings~\citep{su2021roformer} encode position as
rotations applied to query and key vectors, with the rotation frequency
controlled by a base parameter $\theta_{\text{base}}$.
NTK-aware scaling~\citep{bloc97k2023ntk} and YaRN~\citep{peng2023yarn}
interpolate or extrapolate the frequency spectrum to extend context
length beyond the training range. Position Interpolation
(PI)~\citep{chen2023pi} linearly rescales position indices. All three
target \emph{out-of-range} positions; the restart layout in this work
targets a different problem --- \emph{repeated} positions across documents.
The prior rope\_theta sweep used an invalid cached-decode evaluator and is not
interpreted as evidence.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation for attention.}
Self-distillation from a teacher with full context to a student under a
restricted attention pattern has been used to train efficient
encoders~\citep{sanh2020distilbert} and to compress decoder-only models
with sparse attention~\citep{wang2020linformer}. Our self-distillation
recipes
(Section~\ref{sec:results-quality-finetune}) apply the same principle
to block-diagonal attention: the teacher runs full-causal, the student
runs \texttt{ddi\_qf}, and the KL divergence on logits drives the
student toward the teacher's output distribution under the masked path.
The matched Qwen and Llama transfer estimates are reported with their
admissibility and control gates (Section~\ref{sec:results-quality}); the sole
valid natural-QA contrast does not establish a practical architecture
difference.
